From stone to sack, step by step

Every bag you buy goes through these six steps at our plant in Ransigaon, Jodhpur. Plant visits are welcome by appointment.

  1. 1Kiln

    Limestone is burnt to quicklime (CaO) and drawn out onto the conveyor.

  2. 2Raw material stock

    Quicklime from the kiln is stocked under cover and fed to the hydrators by conveyor.

  3. 3Hydrators

    Quicklime is slaked with measured water into dry Ca(OH)2 powder.

  4. 4Classifier

    Separates fine powder from grit to reach each grade's fineness.

  5. 5Small bag packing

    Powder is fed from hoppers and filled in 25, 30, 35 or 40 kg bags.

  6. 6Jumbo-bag packing

    900 kg bags filled under a bag-filter dust collector.

Bad lime costs you money. Here's how we prevent it.

Most lime problems show up in your plant, not on the invoice. These are the five we hear about most often.

Purity lower than promised

You pay for 90 % and dose for 80 %, so consumption goes up.

The grade is a guaranteed minimum

The grade number is the minimum Ca(OH)2 content, and every lot ships with a Certificate of Analysis.

Grit and silica

Inert matter wears pumps and dosing lines and adds sludge.

Silica capped for every grade

Acid insoluble is held as low as 0.8 % in the 95 grade and 1.06 % in the 92 grade, and fineness is up to 99.5 % through 200 mesh.

Lumps and moisture

Damp lime cakes, blocks feeders and loses strength.

Dry powder, sealed bags

Moisture is limited to 0.5 to 2.0 % by grade, and the packing date is printed on every lot.

Every truck is different

One good lot, then the next one upsets your process.

Supplied to your approved sample

We agree the specification against the sample you approved, and every lot is traceable by lot number.

A trader can't answer technical questions or fix a problem.
